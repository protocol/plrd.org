# Impact Methodology v2 — Proposal

> Proposal for review, not implementation-ready.

This proposes a broader rethink of the Impact methodology before `/impact` is taken public.

The main change is to connect **Interventions + Field Velocity** into a single field-building operating model:

**Diagnose → Intervene → Observe → Compound → Learn**

The goal is to make the page more useful as a decision system: diagnose what is holding a field back, choose the appropriate intervention, observe whether that constraint loosens, and feed the learning back into the field.

---

# 1. Diagnose

## What is rate-limiting the field?

Before choosing an intervention, identify what is actually preventing progress.

A constraint might be technical. It might be capital, coordination, talent, infrastructure, demand, policy, legitimacy or institutional capacity.

The useful question is:

> **What is the most important thing preventing this field from moving faster now?**

Typical constraints may include:

* **Knowledge** — we do not yet understand the opportunity, landscape or problem well enough.
* **Capability** — the underlying science or technology cannot yet do what is required.
* **Coordination** — the necessary actors exist, but are fragmented.
* **Talent** — too few capable people are working on the problem.
* **Capital** — the right capital is missing, or available at the wrong stage.
* **Infrastructure** — a shared capability is missing and blocking many actors at once.
* **Validation** — something promising exists, but has not been proven in the real world.
* **Demand** — the capability exists, but there is not yet a clear route to adoption.
* **Policy / legitimacy** — rules, standards, incentives or trust prevent deployment.
* **Institutional capacity** — there is no durable organisation capable of carrying the work forward.

The intervention should follow the diagnosis.

---

# 2. Intervene

## Six repeatable ways to unblock a field

Rather than describing categories of “things PL R&D does”, the intervention taxonomy should describe the repeatable levers by which we try to change conditions in a field.

Each intervention should remain expandable on the website to reveal:

**Failure addressed → What we do → Examples of PL R&D work → Evidence of success**

## Orient

### Make the field legible.

**Failure addressed**

People cannot clearly see the opportunity, understand the landscape, or agree what is stuck.

**What we do**

Field maps · technology trees · Frontier Opportunity memos · benchmarks · taxonomies · roadmaps · research theses · investment theses · agenda-setting research

**What we look for**

Not whether we published something.

Whether credible actors changed what they understood or did.

New programmes form. Funders enter previously neglected areas. Research agendas change. Hidden bottlenecks become visible — and start receiving attention or resources.

---

## Coordinate

### Assemble the actors required to move it.

**Failure addressed**

The right people and institutions exist, but they are fragmented, disconnected or working at cross-purposes.

**What we do**

Decision-grade convenings · scientific advisory boards · funder cohorts · working groups · coalitions · fellowships · cross-sector networks · institutional partnerships

**What we look for**

Repeat collaboration. New projects. Coordinated capital. Agreements or standards. Shorter time from introduction to collaboration.

Most importantly: participants becoming persistent actors in the field.

---

## Resource

### Put resources behind the bottlenecks.

**Failure addressed**

Promising work exists, but lacks the right capital, talent, compute, data or institutional support.

Different stages require different resources.

**Early field formation**
Philanthropy · grants · prizes · fellowships

**Prototype & public goods**
Grants · technical resources · compute · data

**Deployment**
Institutional budgets · public funding · procurement

**Venture conversion**
Venture or growth capital

**Scale**
Revenue · growth capital · durable institutional finance

**What we look for**

Additional capital entering the field. Faster funding. Follow-on resources. A broader capital base.

And whether projects graduate towards the type of capital they actually need next.

---

## Build

### Create the shared capacity the field is missing.

**Failure addressed**

A missing technical capability, piece of infrastructure or institution is blocking progress for many actors at once.

**What we do**

Protocols · open-source tooling · shared datasets · research infrastructure · standards · FROs · nonprofits · institutes · permanent coalitions

**What we look for**

Real users and dependencies.

Lower cost or time for downstream actors. Research that becomes possible because the infrastructure exists. Organisations that spin out.

And, eventually, infrastructure that continues to function without PL R&D.

---

## Prove

### Move promising ideas into the world.

**Failure addressed**

Something promising exists, but lacks credible evidence outside the lab.

**What we do**

Pilots · prototype programmes · sandboxes · demonstrations · reference implementations · deployment partnerships · sovereign pilots · venture conversion

**What we look for**

Evidence.

Pilot → adoption. Procurement. Follow-on deployment. Better cost or performance. Demonstrated decision quality.

The point is to turn:

> “This might work”

into:

> “We now know something we didn’t know before.”

---

## Enable

### Change the environment around what works.

**Failure addressed**

The capability exists, but rules, standards, incentives, legitimacy or institutional structures prevent it from spreading.

**What we do**

Standards · regulatory playbooks · procurement routes · experimentation rights · interoperability rules · assurance frameworks · evaluation frameworks · governance models

**What we look for**

Standards adopted. Procurement enabled. Governments or institutions using the framework. Barriers removed.

And third parties increasingly able to deploy or replicate what works.

---

# Cross-cutting layer

## Narrative · Culture · Talent

Some forces do not fit neatly inside one intervention.

Narrative and culture shape what people notice, understand, believe is possible, choose to join and ultimately sustain.

Talent moves across the entire system: discovering a field, joining networks, receiving resources, building infrastructure, running pilots and creating institutions.

These should sit horizontally across all six interventions rather than as equivalent intervention categories.

---

# 3. Observe

# Field Velocity

## Is the field actually moving?

A field can appear busy without making meaningful progress.

More papers, companies, funding or attention are useful signals, but none is sufficient on its own.

Field Velocity should therefore not be treated as a single score.

Instead, we look for movement across six dimensions:

**Capability · Constraints · Transition Speed · Commitment · Adoption · Durability**

The important question is not only whether activity is increasing.

It is:

> **Where is the field accelerating, what is still holding it back, and is that constraint becoming less binding?**

---

# The six Field Velocity signals

## Capability

### Can the field do more?

We look for changes in what is technically, scientifically or institutionally possible.

**Signals**

Performance benchmarks · cost curves · quality · scale · reliability · new primitives · new experimental capabilities

**Core question**

> What can the field do today that it could not do a year ago?

---

## Constraints

### Are the bottlenecks loosening?

Every field has constraints. Some matter much more than others.

We track the constraints we believe are currently rate-limiting and ask whether they are becoming less binding.

**Signals**

Cost of the constraint · time lost · actors affected · severity · availability of alternatives

**Core question**

> Is the thing that was preventing progress becoming easier to overcome?

---

## Transition Speed

### Is work moving through the field faster?

Velocity should mean something literal.

We measure the time between meaningful stages of progress — and whether those intervals are shrinking.

**Examples**

* idea → result
* result → prototype
* prototype → pilot
* pilot → adoption
* grant → follow-on funding
* introduction → collaboration
* standard → adoption

**Core question**

> Is the path from possibility to real-world use getting shorter?

---

## Commitment

### Are serious actors leaning in?

Attention is cheap.

We care more about what people are willing to commit: time, careers, capital, teams, budgets and institutional capacity.

**Commitment deepens from:**

Attention
→ Participation
→ Resource commitment
→ Institutional commitment
→ Adoption

**Core question**

> Are credible actors making increasingly costly and persistent commitments to the field?

---

## Adoption

### Is useful work reaching the world?

Technical progress is not the same thing as field progress.

We track whether ideas leave the research environment and begin to be used by people, organisations and institutions.

**Signals**

Pilots · active users · production deployments · procurement · standards adoption · repeat deployments · independent replication

**Core question**

> Is what works becoming used?

---

## Durability

### Can the field increasingly move without us?

The strongest field is not one that permanently depends on PL R&D.

It is one that develops its own ability to generate ideas, coordinate actors, fund work, build infrastructure and deploy what works.

**Signals**

Independent funding · durable organisations · maintained infrastructure · recurring collaborations · new programmes · follow-on institutions

**Core question**

> Is the capacity for progress becoming embedded in the field itself?

---

# Measurement instruments

## Signals are not instruments

No single metric works across every field.

We should distinguish between:

**what we are trying to understand**

and

**the instruments we use to observe it**

Different fields will use different combinations.

## Performance curves

How cost, quality, capability or scale changes over time.

Useful where a field has a measurable technical frontier.

## Latency compression

The time between meaningful stages of progress.

Are research, prototyping, deployment and adoption happening faster?

## Idea vintage

How quickly the intellectual frontier is turning over.

Are new results increasingly building on recent discoveries, or does the field keep returning to the same older ideas?

## Revealed commitments

Where people actually put careers, capital, teams and institutional resources.

Not stated interest. Observable commitment.

## Markets

Where sufficiently credible markets exist, they can help us observe changing expectations about future capabilities or events.

Not every field will have one.

## Direct capability measures

Benchmarks or demonstrations showing that something previously impossible is now possible.

## Adoption measures

Pilots, deployments, procurement, usage, standards uptake and independent replication.

The distinction is:

> **Velocity signal = what we want to understand.**

> **Instrument = how we measure it.**

# 4. Inflection points

## What would convince us the field has changed?

Velocity describes movement.

Inflection points describe meaningful changes in state.

For each Frontier Opportunity, define specific shifts that would represent a material change in the field.

Where possible, they should be:

**observable · dated · falsifiable**

For each one, track:

* **Current constraint** — what is preventing progress?
* **Inflection point** — what would constitute a meaningful change?
* **Expected horizon** — when might it plausibly happen?
* **Live signals** — what evidence would tell us we are moving towards it?
* **Relevant interventions** — what are we doing that might help?
* **Status** — what does the evidence currently say?

---

# 5. Compound

## Can progress become self-sustaining?

A successful intervention should do more than produce an isolated outcome.

Over time, we want capabilities, institutions, infrastructure, talent, capital and relationships created in a field to make subsequent progress easier.

We look for evidence that progress is beginning to compound:

**new capability → new actors → new resources → new deployments → stronger field**

The final test is not how many PL R&D programmes exist.

It is whether the field develops more capacity to move on its own.

That means:

* more people able to orient the field
* more actors coordinating without us
* more sources of capital
* more shared infrastructure
* more routes from research to deployment
* more institutions capable of carrying the work forward

Our north-star question is:

> **Is the field becoming increasingly capable of generating, coordinating, funding, deploying and sustaining progress itself?**

Successful field building should eventually make the field **less dependent on the field builder**.

---

# 6. Learn

## Every intervention is a hypothesis

We do not know in advance that an intervention will work.

For meaningful programmes, the underlying assumption should be explicit:

> **We believe [constraint] is holding back [field].**

> **We are applying [intervention] because we expect [observable change].**

> **We will use [evidence] to test whether that happened.**

Then revisit it.

* Did the constraint loosen?
* Did the expected change happen?
* Did another bottleneck become more important?
* Was our original diagnosis wrong?
* What should we do next?

The learning feeds back into the next diagnosis:

**constraint → intervention → expected change → observed evidence → updated diagnosis**

The aim is not to prove ourselves right.

It is to get better at building fields.

---

# 7. Verified Impact + Hypercerts

## Documenting our hand

Field Velocity describes what is happening in the field.

Verified Impact documents what PL R&D actually did.

Keep **Hypercerts** as a core part of Verified Impact, alongside programme records, outputs and supporting evidence.

The purpose is to make our contribution legible and inspectable without pretending that field-level outcomes can be cleanly attributed to PL R&D.

A useful distinction:

> **Field Velocity describes what is happening in the field.**

> **Verified Impact documents our hand.**

Verified Impact should continue to include:

* programme records
* outputs
* supporting evidence
* Hypercerts
* intervention records
* evidence timelines where useful

Together, this lets us document:

**what we did → what happened next → what evidence exists → what we learned**

This preserves the existing attribution humility while still making PL R&D’s contribution legible.

# Proposed page architecture

## Hero

**How we build fields**

PL R&D works on frontier fields where the opportunity is large, but progress is constrained by one or more factors.

We map the field, identify what is holding it back, and intervene where we believe we can unlock the most movement.

Then we watch what changes.

Fields are complex systems, with many actors. We make our hypotheses explicit, document and measure our interventions, and look for evidence that the constraints we targeted are beginning to loosen.

**Diagnose → Intervene → Observe → Compound → Learn**

Our goal is to help frontier fields become increasingly capable of generating and sustaining progress themselves.

---

## Diagnose

*What is preventing the field from moving?*

We build a picture of the field: its capabilities, actors, resources, infrastructure, incentives and bottlenecks.

The objective is to identify the binding constraints — the things that, if changed, could unlock disproportionate progress.

---

## Intervene

*What could unlock movement?*

We use six repeatable intervention types:

**Orient → Coordinate → Resource → Build → Prove → Enable**

These describe what we are trying to cause in a field:

**Orient**
Make the field legible.

**Coordinate**
Bring the right actors into alignment.

**Resource**
Move capital, talent and support toward important work.

**Build**
Create missing capabilities, infrastructure or institutions.

**Prove**
Demonstrate what works in the real world.

**Enable**
Remove barriers to adoption, replication and scale.

Across these interventions, three forces often cut horizontally through the field:

**Narrative · Culture · Talent**

---

## Observe

*Is the field moving?*

We look for evidence that the constraints we targeted are loosening and that the field is becoming more capable of generating progress.

We call this **Field Velocity**.

Rather than relying on a single measure, we look across six dimensions:

**Capability**
Can the field do more than it could before?

**Constraints**
Are the bottlenecks preventing progress becoming less binding?

**Transition Speed**
Is work moving faster from idea → research → development → deployment?

**Commitment**
Are credible actors committing meaningful time, talent, capital or institutional support?

**Adoption**
Is useful work being taken up beyond its original creators?

**Durability**
Is the field developing the capacity to sustain progress without continued intervention from us?

### Measuring Field Velocity

The dimensions describe **what we want to understand**. The instruments describe **how we measure it**.

Depending on the field, we use a combination of:

**Performance curves · Latency compression · Idea vintage · Revealed commitments · Markets · Direct capability measures · Adoption measures**

We do not expect every measure to move at once. We look for multiple signals that, taken together, indicate whether the field is changing.

### Inflection points

We also define the specific changes that would indicate a meaningful shift in the field.

For each important constraint, we track:

**Current constraint → Inflection point → Expected horizon → Live signals → Relevant interventions → Status**

Where possible, these should be **observable, dated and falsifiable**.

This gives us a live view of what needs to change next, whether it is changing, and whether our interventions appear relevant to that movement.
